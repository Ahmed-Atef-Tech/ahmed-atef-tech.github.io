Featured project

A safe way for a small computer to switch real appliances.

Version 1 built, version 2 in progress

Isolated 4-channel AC controller

A microcontroller runs on a few volts, while lamps and machines run on 220 V mains. This board sits between them. It lets the controller turn four appliances on and off, while keeping the low-voltage side completely separated from mains for safety.

  • Switches four 220 V loads, up to 8 A each
  • Every channel has its own fuse, plus surge protection on the input
  • The board reports back when each output is actually on

Testing the first version showed things to improve, and version 2 fixes them.

How I work

From an idea to a working board, step by step.

1

Understand the job

What should the device do, where will it live, and what must it never do? Everything else follows from this.

2

Draw the circuit

I choose the parts and draw the schematic, then check the tricky parts in simulation before anything gets built.

3

Lay out the board

I place every part and route every connection, keeping it safe, compact and easy for a factory to make.

4

Build and test

The first boards come back and get tested on the bench. What I learn goes straight into the next version.

5

Put it in the product

The board gets its enclosure, mounts and wiring, so it becomes part of a real machine.
